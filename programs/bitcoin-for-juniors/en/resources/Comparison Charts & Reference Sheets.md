## **Chapter 3: Monetary Systems — Fiat vs. Sound Money**

| Aspect | Sound Money (Gold/Commodity) | Fiat Money (Government Decree) |
|--------|------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| **Backing** | Physical commodity (gold, silver) | Government decree + trust      |
| **Supply** | Limited by scarcity of commodity | Unlimited; can be printed      |
| **Inflation** | Low (commodity supply grows slowly) | High (governments print more)  |
| **Who controls?** | Market determines value; no single authority | Central bank + government      |
| **Purchasing power** | Relatively stable over decades | Erodes over time               |
| **Can be debased?** | Difficult (commodity is commodity) | Easy (print more, reduce value per unit) |
| **Security** | Based on physical scarcity   | Based on trust in authority    |

## **Chapter 7: On-Chain vs. Lightning Network**

|---------|------------------|-------------------|
| **Speed** | \~10 minutes     | Seconds           |
| **Cost** | $1–30            | <$0.01            |

## **Chapter 9: Mining Economics — Incentive Structure**

### **Miner Revenue**

```
Block Reward (fixed) = 6.25 BTC (halves every 4 years)
Transaction Fees (variable) = Sum of fees in block
Total Revenue = 6.25 BTC + Fees
```

### **Miner Costs**

```
Hardware: $5,000–$100,000+ (amortized over years)
Electricity: $0.02–0.05 per kWh (varies by region)
Cooling: Significant for large operations
Pool Fees: 1-2% of earnings (if in mining pool)
```

### **Incentive Alignment**

* If Bitcoin price is high → mining profitable → more miners join → difficulty increases → less profitable until price goes up or miners exit
* Boom-bust cycle naturally regulates participation
* Security cost = electricity spent; makes 51% attack exponentially expensive

## **Chapter 10: CBDC vs. Bitcoin — Fundamental Differences**

| Aspect | CBDC (Government Digital Currency) | Bitcoin |
|--------|------------------------------------|---------|
| **Issuer** | Central bank                       | Decentralized network (no issuer) |
| **Supply** | Unlimited; government controls     | Fixed 21 million; algorithm controls |
| **Control** | Government can freeze accounts, reverse transactions | No authority can stop transactions |
| **Privacy** | Government can see all transactions | Pseudonymous; transparent on chain |
| **Accessibility** | Requires central bank system; can exclude | Anyone with internet can participate |
| **Programmable** | Yes; government sets rules         | Limited; network consensus required |
| **Censorship Risk** | High; government can freeze/block  | Low; decentralized consensus |
| **Financial Sovereignty** | False (you use government's system) | True (you control your money) |
