## **Chapter 2: The History of Money — Local Examples**

### Prompt for Teachers:

Before teaching, research **one local example** from your region's monetary history.

**Questions to answer:**

* What served as money 100+ years ago in your community?
* Why did it work as money? (property analysis)
* What caused the transition to the next form?
* Can any older people (grandparents) share stories of past monetary systems?

**Example structure:**

> **Cattle in East Africa** — Pastoralist societies used livestock as money. Durable (survived), portable (herds move), scarce (hard to breed quickly), fungible (one cow ≈ another). Transition: as communities grew and trade became complex, cattle became impractical → moved to currency.

## **Chapter 4: How Problems Lead to Solutions — Inflation Case Studies**

### **Venezuela (2013–present)**

* **Purchasing power collapse:** In 2012, monthly wage bought groceries for a family. By 2020, same wage bought one meal.
* **Currency debasement:** Government printed money to pay debts; hyperinflation followed.
* **Bitcoin adoption:** Some Venezuelans turned to Bitcoin to preserve savings from currency collapse.

### **Zimbabwe (2008)**

* **Hyperinflation:** Prices doubled every 24 hours at peak; prices on menus changed during the day.
* **Currency became worthless:** Citizens switched to US dollars for survival.
* **Lesson:** Fiat money can become valueless if government abuses printing.

### **Turkey (2020–2023)**

* **Currency collapse:** Turkish lira lost 80% of value in 3 years due to high inflation.
* **Wage stagnation:** Worker salaries didn't keep pace; purchasing power halved.
* **Bitcoin interest surged:** Bitcoin searches and adoption increased as citizens sought hedge.

## **Chapter 7: Using Bitcoin in Daily Life — Merchant & Adoption Stories**

### **El Salvador (Bitcoin Law, Sept 2021)**

* **Policy:** First country to adopt Bitcoin as legal tender alongside US dollar.
* **Merchant adoption:** Chivo wallet (government BTC wallet) used in stores; Bitcoin ATMs installed.
* **Remittances:** \~20% of El Salvador's GDP comes from migrants sending money home; Bitcoin reduces fees from 8-10% to <1%.

### **Circular Economy: Austin, Texas**

* **The Loop:** Bitcoin-accepting businesses (coffee, restaurants, services) circulate Bitcoin within community.
* **No conversion needed:** Customer buys coffee with Bitcoin → shop pays employee in Bitcoin → employee buys lunch with Bitcoin.
* **Benefit:** Eliminates fees; keeps Bitcoin in local economy.

### **Lightning Adoption: Small Merchants**

* **Coffee shop in Panama:** Accepts Lightning payments; transactions settle in <1 second for <$0.01 fee.
* **Contrast:** Credit card processing takes 2-3 days; merchant pays 2-3% fee.

## **Chapter 10: What Future Can Bitcoin Build — Impact Stories**

### **Remittance Worker in Philippines**

* **Problem:** Sending $200 to family costs $20+ in bank fees (10% loss); takes 3-5 days.
* **Bitcoin solution:** Sends $200 via Bitcoin; cost <$0.50; arrives in 10 minutes; family receives full amount.
* **Annual impact:** If sending monthly, saves $240/year (enough for one child's school fees).

### **Banking the Unbanked: Mobile Money in El Salvador**

* **Baseline:** 70% of population had no bank account; no way to save or access credit.
* **Bitcoin/Chivo wallet:** Anyone with phone can now hold Bitcoin; access global market; receive remittances.
* **Agency:** For first time, citizens not dependent on government-controlled banking system.

### **Hyperinflation Escape: Lebanese Business Owner**

* **Problem:** Lebanese pound lost 90% of value; government imposed capital controls (can't withdraw own money).
* **Bitcoin solution:** Moved savings to Bitcoin; stored on seed phrase (can't be frozen or confiscated).
* **Freedom:** Despite currency crisis at home, retained purchasing power and access to capital.
