## **Early Adopters (2010-2015)**

### **Laszlo Hanyecz — The First Bitcoin Purchase (2010)**

* **Story:** Programmer bought two Papa John's pizzas for 10,000 BTC (May 22, 2010)
* **Value then:** \~$25
* **Value now:** \~$400 million
* **Significance:** Proved Bitcoin could work as medium of exchange; every May 22, Bitcoin community celebrates "Pizza Day"
* **Lesson:** Bitcoin works; adoption spreads through use, not speculation

## **Merchants & Businesses (2014-2020)**

### **Microsoft — Enterprise Adoption (2014)**

* **Decision:** Accepted Bitcoin for Xbox games and apps
* **Impact:** Legitimized Bitcoin as payment method for mainstream company
* **Timeline:** Discontinued in 2018 but shows institutional exploration

### **Overstock.com — Full Merchant Integration**

* **Adoption:** Accepted Bitcoin for purchases across entire store (2014)
* **Stayed committed:** Unlike many merchants, Overstock kept Bitcoin acceptance
* **CEO statement:** Saw Bitcoin as future; invested company surplus in BTC

## **Communities & Remittance Networks (2017-2023)**

### **El Salvador — Bitcoin as Legal Tender (Sept 2021)**

* **Policy:** President announced Bitcoin legal tender alongside US dollar
* **Remittances:** \~20% of GDP comes from diaspora sending money home; Bitcoin cuts fees from 8-10% to <1%
* **Chivo Wallet:** Government-issued wallet with $30 bonus to encourage adoption
* **Reality:** Mixed adoption; some merchants refused or converted immediately to dollars; network congestion issues early on
* **Lesson:** Government mandate doesn't guarantee organic adoption; economic incentives matter more

### **The Philippines — OFW (Overseas Filipino Worker) Remittances**

* **Context:** 12 million Filipinos send money home; $37+ billion annually
* **Problem:** Banks charge 5-7% fees; 2-3 day delays
* **Bitcoin solution:** Faster, cheaper; some OFWs use Bitcoin to send
* **Current adoption:** Growing but still minority; stablecoins (USDT) more popular due to price stability

## **Regional Communities & Circular Economies**

### **Austin, Texas — The Loop**

* **Definition:** Network of Bitcoin-accepting businesses (coffee, restaurants, services)
* **How it works:** Customer pays in Bitcoin → shop pays employee in Bitcoin → employee buys lunch with Bitcoin (no conversion needed)
* **Benefit:** Keeps Bitcoin circulating; eliminates conversion fees; builds community
* **Status:** Active but still niche; most businesses accept but don't exclusively use Bitcoin

### **Miami — Bitcoin-Friendly City**

* **Mayor Francis Suarez:** Proactively recruited Bitcoin companies; city accepts Bitcoin for taxes
* **Impact:** FTX headquarters (until collapse), Bitcoin conference hub, Bitcoin mining initiatives
* **Lesson:** Government support accelerates adoption; regulatory clarity attracts business

## **Large Institutions (2020-2024)**

### **MicroStrategy — Corporate Treasury Adoption**

* **Move:** Company purchased Bitcoin as treasury reserve (\~$3+ billion worth)
* **CEO Michael Saylor:** Bitcoin is "corporate equivalent of gold"
* **Impact:** Signaled that public companies could hold Bitcoin without shareholder revolt
* **Copycat effect:** Other companies (Tesla, Square) followed

### **El Salvador Government Bonds**

* **2021:** El Salvador issued first Bitcoin-backed government bond
* **Failed:** But showed institutional interest in Bitcoin-backed instruments

## **Use Cases Emerging (2022-2024)**

### **Lightning Network Adoption**

* **Panama City Coffee Shop:** Accepts Lightning payments; transaction settles in <1 second for <$0.01
* **El Salvador Chivo Wallet:** Citizens in rural areas use Lightning for daily transactions
* **Advantage over on-chain:** Scalable for micropayments

### **Stablecoin Adoption (USDT, USDC)**

* **Context:** Bitcoin adoption slower than stablecoin adoption for remittances/payments
* **Why:** Stablecoins pegged to dollar = no price volatility; Bitcoin's volatility keeps it store-of-value focused
* **Lesson:** For payment use case, stability matters more than decentralization (at least for now)
