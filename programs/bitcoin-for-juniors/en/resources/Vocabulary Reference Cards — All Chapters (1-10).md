## **Chapter 1: What Is Money?**

| Term | Definition | Example |
|------|------------|---------|
| **Money** | A tool widely accepted in exchange that solves the problem of barter | Dollar bill, Bitcoin |
| **Barter** | Direct exchange of goods without using money | Trading a sandwich for a pencil |
| **Store of Value** | Money that keeps its worth over time so you can save it | Saving dollars in a piggy bank |
| **Medium of Exchange** | Something everyone accepts so you can trade it for whatever you need | Using money to buy anything in a shop |
| **Unit of Account** | A standard measure that lets you compare value of different things | Price of rice: 50 cents per kilo |
| **Durability** | Money that lasts through use and time without breaking | Metal coins last longer than paper |
| **Divisibility** | Ability to break money into smaller pieces for small purchases | Breaking a $10 bill into coins |
| **Portability** | How easily you can carry money from place to place | Coins fit in a pocket; a cow does not |
| **Acceptability** | Everyone willing to take this in exchange | Everyone takes the national currency |
| **Scarcity** | Limited supply; you can't create it infinitely | Gold is scarce; sand is not |
| **Fungibility** | Each unit is identical and interchangeable with any other unit | Any dollar bill worth same as any other |
| **Commodity Money** | Money made from or backed by a valuable physical good | Gold coins, shells, salt |
| **Representative Money** | Paper or digital money that represents a claim on something real | Gold certificates, bank checks |
| **Fiat Money** | Money that has value because a government says it does, not backed by commodity | Modern dollar bills |
| **Digital Currency** | Money that exists only as data, not as physical coins or bills | Mobile money, Bitcoin |
| **Double Coincidence of Wants** | In barter, both people must have what the other wants at same time | Fisherman has fish, wants cloth; tailor has cloth, wants goats |
| **Trade-off** | Giving up one thing to get another because you can't have both | Spending money on phone means you can't spend it on a trip |

## **Chapter 2: The History of Money**

| Term | Definition | Example |
|------|------------|---------|
| **Barter System** | Economy based on direct exchange of goods without money | Village where farmers trade food for tools |
| **Commodity Money** | Physical objects widely valued and accepted as money | Gold, silver, shells, cattle |
| **Coinage** | Metal money stamped with official markings for authenticity | Roman denarius, medieval coins |
| **Debasement** | Reducing the metal content of coins to profit from the difference | Melting down gold coins, replacing with cheaper metal |
| **Paper Receipt** | Early form of representative money; paper claim on stored metal | Gold certificate you could exchange for actual gold |
| **Gold Standard** | Monetary system where currency is directly backed by gold | Before 1971, US dollar was backed by gold at $35/ounce |
| **Sound Money** | Money backed by a scarce physical commodity (usually precious metals) | Gold, silver |
| **Unsound Money** | Money not backed by a physical commodity; value based on government decree | Paper currency, fiat money |
| **Bretton Woods Agreement** | 1944 agreement fixing world currencies to the US dollar | Dollar pegged to gold; other currencies pegged to dollar |
| **Nixon Shock** | 1971 moment when US ended dollar-to-gold convertibility | End of gold standard; beginning of fully fiat money |
| **Monetary Evolution** | How money changed over time from barter to digital | Progression: barter → commodity → coins → paper → digital |
| **Portability** | How easy money is to carry and transport | Gold was heavy; paper money more portable |
| **Trust in Money** | Confidence that money will maintain its value and be accepted | People must believe in the currency |

## **Chapter 3: What Is Fiat Money?**

| Term | Definition | Example |
|------|------------|---------|
| **Fiat** | Latin term meaning "by decree"; something ordered into existence by authority | Fiat money has value because government declares it legal tender |
| **Legal Tender Law** | Law requiring citizens to accept a specific currency as payment | US law requiring acceptance of US dollars |
| **Fiat Money** | Currency not backed by commodity; value based on government decree and trust | Modern dollar, euro, peso |
| **Central Bank** | Government institution that controls money supply and banking system | Federal Reserve (US), European Central Bank |
| **Fractional Reserve Banking** | Banks legally lend out most deposits; keep only a fraction in reserve | Bank receives $100, keeps $10, lends out $90 |
| **Money Multiplication** | How fractional reserve banking creates new money through lending | $100 deposit becomes $1,000+ through multiple loans |
| **Reserve Requirement** | Minimum percentage of deposits banks must hold (not lend out) | 10% reserve means bank can lend 90% of deposits |
| **Monetary Policy** | Central bank actions to control money supply and interest rates | Adjusting interest rates, quantitative easing |
| **Inflation** | General rise in prices; decline in purchasing power of money | Prices rising means your money buys less |
| **Interest Rate** | Cost of borrowing money; rate at which banks lend to each other | Central banks set benchmark interest rates |
| **Boom-Bust Cycle** | Pattern of credit expansion (boom) followed by contraction (bust) | Economic periods of growth followed by recession/depression |
| **Bank Run** | Everyone tries to withdraw money from bank at same time | Bank doesn't have enough cash because it lent out most deposits |
| **Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC)** | Fully digital form of fiat currency issued and controlled by central bank | Digital version of US dollar or euro |
| **Centralized Control** | Single entity (government, central bank) controls the money system | Contrast to Bitcoin's decentralized network |

## **Chapter 4: How Problems Lead to Solutions**

| Term | Definition | Example |
|------|------------|---------|
| **Purchasing Power** | How much goods and services your money can buy | If $100 buys 100 loaves in Year 1, but only 80 loaves in Year 2, purchasing power declined |
| **Monetary Inflation** | Increase in money supply in the economy | Central bank creating new money through lending |
| **Price Inflation** | General rise in prices of goods and services | Loaf of bread costs $2 today, $3 tomorrow |
| **Debt Burden** | Total amount of money owed across economy (government, businesses, individuals) | National debt, mortgages, student loans accumulating |
| **Wealth Inequality** | Gap between rich and poor; unequal distribution of wealth | Top 1% owns most assets while wages stagnate |
| **Cypherpunks** | Group of activists advocating for privacy and cryptography in the 1980s-1990s | Worked on early digital cash before Bitcoin |
| **Decentralization** | Distributing control across many participants instead of one central authority | Bitcoin network vs. central bank control |
| **Centralized System** | Single entity (government, bank) controls the system | Traditional banking, fiat money |
| **Decentralized System** | Control distributed among many participants; no single authority | Bitcoin network, peer-to-peer systems |
| **Censorship Resistance** | System cannot be shut down or transactions blocked by central authority | Bitcoin can't be stopped by government decree |
| **Financial Sovereignty** | Ability to control your own money without intermediaries | Self-custody of Bitcoin vs. bank holding your money |
| **Sound Money** | Money with fixed supply that can't be inflated away | Gold, Bitcoin (vs. fiat) |
| **Time Preference** | People prefer to have something NOW rather than later | Would you take $100 today or $110 in a year? |

## **Chapter 5: What Is Bitcoin?**

| Term | Definition | Example |
|------|------------|---------|
| **Bitcoin** | Decentralized peer-to-peer digital money; first successful cryptocurrency | The original blockchain-based currency created in 2009 |
| **Satoshi Nakamoto** | Anonymous creator(s) of Bitcoin; published white paper in October 2008 | Identity still unknown |
| **Bitcoin Whitepaper** | Technical document explaining how Bitcoin works; titled "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System" | October 31, 2008 publication |
| **Peer-to-Peer** | Direct communication between parties without intermediaries | Bitcoin transactions don't need a bank |
| **Decentralized** | No single point of control; network of many computers | Contrast to centralized bank |
| **Consensus Rules** | Rules that all Bitcoin participants agree to follow | Fixed supply of 21 million Bitcoin, 10-minute block time |
| **Nakamoto Consensus** | Mechanism where miners solve math puzzles to create blocks and earn Bitcoin | Proof of Work system securing the network |
| **Blockchain** | Chain of blocks containing transaction records; permanent record | Bitcoin's ledger of all transactions |
| **Block** | Container of transactions; added to blockchain roughly every 10 minutes | Contains multiple transactions and proof of work |
| **Mining** | Process of creating new Bitcoin blocks and validating transactions | Miners compete to solve math puzzles for block rewards |
| **Miner** | Computer solving math puzzles to create new blocks | Receives block reward (new Bitcoin) + transaction fees |
| **Node** | Computer running Bitcoin software that validates transactions | Maintains copy of entire blockchain |
| **Fixed Supply** | Bitcoin has maximum of 21 million; cannot be inflated | Key difference from fiat money |
| **Scarce Digital Money** | Digital money with verifiable scarcity (no counterfeits possible) | Bitcoin's main innovation |
| **Sound Digital Money** | Money that's scarce, durable, divisible, portable, acceptable, fungible | Bitcoin meets all criteria of good money |

## **Chapter 6: How to Use Bitcoin**

| Term | Definition | Example |
|------|------------|---------|
| **Bitcoin Wallet** | Software that stores your private keys and displays your balance | Blue Wallet, Muun, Ledger |
| **Private Key** | Secret number that proves you own Bitcoin and authorizes transactions | 256-bit number; must be kept secret |
| **Public Address** | Your Bitcoin address where others can send Bitcoin to you | Looks like: 1A1z7agoat2BYJU7KSnLc127z8LHZLi7m |
| **Seed Phrase** | 12 or 24 words that can recreate all your Bitcoin wallets | Backup for your wallet; keep it secret and safe |
| **QR Code** | Visual code representing your Bitcoin address; scanned with phone camera | Easier than typing long address manually |
| **Self-Custody** | You hold and control your own private keys | You are responsible for backup and security |
| **Custodial Wallet** | Third party (exchange, bank) holds your private keys for you | Easier but less control; requires trust |
| **Non-Custodial Wallet** | You control your own private keys; third party cannot access your Bitcoin | Maximum security but user responsible for backup |
| **Hardware Wallet** | Physical device that stores private keys offline | Ledger, Trezor (more secure than mobile) |
| **Mobile Wallet** | Phone app that stores private keys on your phone | Blue Wallet, Muun (convenient, less secure than hardware) |
| **UTXO** | Unspent Transaction Output; the Bitcoin you own | Think of it like cash bills in your wallet |
| **Transaction Fee** | Amount you pay to miners to include your transaction in a block | Higher fee = faster confirmation |
| **Confirmation** | Transaction added to a block and confirmed by the network | More confirmations = more secure |
| **Private Key Security** | Never share your seed phrase or private key with anyone | If someone has it, they can steal your Bitcoin |

## **Chapter 7: Using Bitcoin in Daily Life**

| Term | Definition | Example |
|------|------------|---------|
| **Lightning Network** | Layer 2 payment system on top of Bitcoin; enables fast, cheap transactions | Sending Bitcoin for coffee in seconds |
| **Payment Channel** | Direct connection between two Lightning users allowing multiple transactions | Alice ↔ Bob channel; they can transact without blockchain |
| **Routing** | How Lightning payments travel through network of channels to reach recipient | Payment goes Alice → Carol → Bob (through intermediaries) |
| **Layer 2** | System built on top of Bitcoin that increases transaction speed | Lightning is Layer 2; on-chain Bitcoin is Layer 1 |
| **On-chain Transaction** | Bitcoin transaction recorded directly on blockchain | Slower (10 min), more secure, higher fee |
| **Lightning Transaction** | Bitcoin transaction via Lightning Network | Faster (seconds), lower fee, instant |
| **Circular Economy** | Economic system where Bitcoin circulates within a community | Coffee shop accepts Bitcoin → pays employee → buys supplies with Bitcoin |
| **Merchant Adoption** | Businesses accepting Bitcoin as payment | El Salvador law requiring merchants to accept Bitcoin |
| **Point of Sale (POS)** | System for accepting payment at checkout | Bitcoin QR code scanned at register |
| **Bitcoin ATM** | Machine that exchanges Bitcoin for local currency (and vice versa) | Used in cities where Bitcoin adoption is high |
| **Remittance** | Money sent from one country to another (usually by migrant workers) | Worker sends Bitcoin home to family; avoids bank fees |
| **Cross-Border Payment** | Sending money between countries | Bitcoin faster and cheaper than traditional wire transfer |
| **Fee Structure** | Comparison of costs across payment methods | Bitcoin on-chain: $1-30 / Lightning: <$0.01 / Bank wire: $15-50 |
| **BTCPay Server** | Open-source Bitcoin payment processor for merchants | Merchants accept Bitcoin without fees or third-party control |

## **Chapter 8: How Bitcoin Works (Technical)**

| Term | Definition | Example |
|------|------------|---------|
| **Public Key Cryptography** | System where you have a public key (shareable) and private key (secret) | Allows secure transactions without trusting intermediary |
| **Hash Function** | Algorithm that converts any input into fixed-length output; one-way | SHA-256 hash always produces 64-character output |
| **Hashing** | Running data through a hash function to create a fingerprint | Bitcoin block contains hash of previous block |
| **Merkle Tree** | Structure of hashes that efficiently summarizes all transactions in a block | Allows quick verification of transaction inclusion |
| **Digital Signature** | Proof that you authorized a transaction without revealing private key | Uses your private key to sign, public key to verify |
| **UTXO Model** | Bitcoin accounting method where you own "coins" (UTXOs) | Contrast to account-based systems (bank balance) |
| **Input** | UTXO you're spending in a transaction | The Bitcoin you're sending |
| **Output** | New UTXO created by transaction (split between recipient and change) | Where Bitcoin goes after transaction |
| **Change Address** | New address receiving leftover Bitcoin after transaction | If you send $8 from a $10 UTXO, $2 goes to change address |
| **Confirmation** | Transaction included in a block and added to blockchain | More confirmations = transaction is more final |
| **Block Header** | Metadata of a block including timestamp, previous block hash, Merkle root | Contains key info for verifying block validity |
| **Immutability** | Characteristic that past transactions cannot be changed | Older blocks become exponentially harder to alter |
| **Blockchain** | Ledger of all Bitcoin transactions in chronological order | Called "blockchain" because blocks are chained together with hashes |
| **Cryptographic Security** | Security based on math; not trusting people or institutions | Bitcoin is secure by math, not by who controls it |

## **Chapter 9: How Does Bitcoin Mining Work?**

| Term | Definition | Example |
|------|------------|---------|
| **Mining** | Competitive process of solving math puzzles to create new Bitcoin blocks | Miners race to find valid block hash first |
| **Miner** | Computer or person operating hardware that mines Bitcoin | Solo miner or member of mining pool |
| **Proof of Work** | Demonstrating that work was done (solving hash puzzle) | Proves block creator spent computational resources |
| **Hash Puzzle** | Cryptographic puzzle miners must solve to create valid block | Finding a hash that meets difficulty target |
| **Difficulty Target** | Number miners must hash below to create valid block; gets harder as network grows | Automatically adjusts so blocks come \~every 10 minutes |
| **Block Reward** | New Bitcoin and transaction fees given to miner who creates valid block | 6.25 BTC (will halve again in 2028) + fees |
| **Transaction Fee** | Payment from user to miner for including transaction in block | Higher fee = higher priority |
| **Mempool** | Waiting area for unconfirmed transactions; miners choose which to include | Transactions ordered by fee rate |
| **Block Time** | Average time between blocks (Bitcoin targets \~10 minutes) | If blocks are faster, difficulty adjusts up |
| **Difficulty Adjustment** | Automatic recalibration of hash puzzle difficulty every 2,016 blocks (\~2 weeks) | Keeps block time consistent as hash power changes |
| **Hash Rate** | Total computational power of Bitcoin network | Measured in hashes per second; higher = more secure |
| **51% Attack** | Theoretical attack where someone controls 51%+ of hash power | Would require enormous electricity and hardware cost |
| **Nakamoto Consensus** | Agreement mechanism where majority hash power determines valid chain | Makes Bitcoin secure without central authority |
| **Double Spending** | Attempting to spend same Bitcoin twice | Miners prevent this by enforcing transaction order in blocks |

## **Chapter 10: What Future Can Bitcoin Build?**

| Term | Definition | Example |
|------|------------|---------|
| **Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC)** | Fully digital government-issued currency controlled by central bank | Digital yuan, digital euro (in development) |
| **Censorship Resistance** | System that cannot block or reverse transactions by government decree | Bitcoin cannot be stopped by any authority |
| **Financial Sovereignty** | Ability to control and move your own money without permission | Bitcoin lets you be your own bank |
| **Permissionless** | Anyone can participate without approval from authority | Bitcoin: anyone can send, receive, mine |
| **Permissioned** | Requires approval from authority to participate | Banks control who can have account |
| **Sound Money** | Money with fixed supply that resists debasement | Bitcoin (fixed 21M supply) vs. fiat (unlimited supply) |
| **Unsound Money** | Money that can be inflated by authorities at will | Fiat currency; purchasing power continuously erodes |
| **Hyperinflation** | Extreme, rapid inflation where currency becomes nearly worthless | Venezuela (2+ million % annually), Zimbabwe, Argentina |
| **Currency Crisis** | Loss of confidence in a currency causing collapse in value | Lebanon lira, Turkish lira, others |
| **Banking the Unbanked** | Providing financial services to people without bank accounts | Bitcoin accessible to anyone with phone + internet |
| **Remittance Corridor** | Path of money sent from migrant workers to home country | Philippines, Mexico, El Salvador receive large remittances |
| **Economic Freedom** | Ability to control, move, save your money without state interference | Bitcoin enables this globally |
| **Digital Money** | Money existing only as data, not physical form | Bitcoin, CBDCs, bank deposits all digital |
| **Sound Digital Money** | Digital money with verifiable scarcity and full control | Bitcoin is the first truly sound digital money |
