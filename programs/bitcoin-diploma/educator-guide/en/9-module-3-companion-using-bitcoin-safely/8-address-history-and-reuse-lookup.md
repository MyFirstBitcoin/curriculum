# 9.8 Address history and reuse lookup

This activity makes public-ledger privacy visible.

**Purpose:** Show that address activity may be visible and that repeated address use can make transactions easier to link.

**Materials:** A deliberately public, educator-prepared address or transaction; a local-node interface, optional explorer, or dated printed screenshots; privacy questions.

**Safety boundary:** Never ask learners to search, display, or share an address or transaction belonging to them, a classmate, a private individual, or an unconsenting organization. Searching an address through a third-party explorer can itself reveal interest in that address.

**Facilitation:**

1. State why the example is safe and public.
1. Ask learners what they expect an observer can see.
1. Identify transactions, amounts, timing, or reuse visible in the prepared example.
1. Ask whether the address appears to have received more than once.
1. Discuss what an observer can infer and what they cannot know with certainty.
1. Show that many wallets generate a fresh receiving address.
1. Explain that old addresses may remain valid, but fresh addresses improve privacy by reducing easy linkage.

**Debrief:**

* Does an address reveal a legal identity by itself?
* How could combining public data with other information change the answer?
* Does using a fresh address make Bitcoin private in every respect?
* Why should an educator avoid searching a learner's address on a public website?

**Offline fallback:** Use a printed sequence showing one address used once and another reused across several receipts.
