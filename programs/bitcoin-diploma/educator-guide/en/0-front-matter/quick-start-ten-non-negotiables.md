# Quick start: ten non-negotiables

These are guardrails, not a test. Keep this list open while you prepare. You do not need to memorize the curriculum before teaching; you do need to protect these boundaries every time.

1. Teach the four modules in sequence: Understanding Money, How Bitcoin Works, Using Bitcoin Safely, and Bitcoin in Your Community.
1. Treat Modules 1–3 as the shared Core. Module 4 is required, but its local learning task is educator-selected.
1. Preserve the approved objectives, evidence requirements, safety boundaries, and passing standard in every language and delivery mode.
1. Conduct approved educator-led theory interactions and direct practical observation. Offer the app only as an optional recap, self-check, or discovery resource.
1. Give supported practice before assessment. For practical learning, use Presentation → Practice → Production.
1. Never ask to see, handle, photograph, copy, record, or store a learner's real seed phrase, private key, credential, balance, or unnecessary transaction information.
1. Use prepared wallets, simulations, test environments, printed screens, or appropriately low-stakes practice. Always prepare a no-funds or offline fallback.
1. Do not give financial, investment, legal, tax, accounting, compliance, or trading advice. Do not pressure learners to acquire or use bitcoin.
1. Treat named wallets, exchanges, services, maps, merchants, and community resources as dated local examples, not global recommendations.
1. Do not certify attendance, app activity, an educator interaction without valid evidence, or either assessment component alone. Certification requires approved educator-led theoretical and practical evidence.
