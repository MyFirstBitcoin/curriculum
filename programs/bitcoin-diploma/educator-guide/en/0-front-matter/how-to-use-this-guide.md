# How to use this guide

Use the guide in three passes.

1. **Before the course:** Read the course principles, planning chapters, assessment model, and Module 4 selection guidance. Complete the preparation and local verification records.
1. **Before each module:** Use the relevant module companion to choose a lesson flow, prepare questions and materials, anticipate misconceptions, and identify the evidence you need.
1. **During and after delivery:** Use the facilitation patterns, safety procedures, assessment process, feedback guidance, and record templates.

Do not try to use every prompt or activity. Select the smallest combination that enables learners to meet the objectives safely. Add time when the learning, practice, or assessment requires it.

### The page-by-page teaching rhythm

When you open a module companion, follow the same order every time:

1. **Purpose:** know why this module matters.
1. **Learner promise:** tell learners what they should be able to do by the end.
1. **Prepare before class:** gather materials, check local facts, and test any demonstration.
1. **Suggested flow:** use the timing as a starting point, not a test of your teaching.
1. **Questions and activities:** choose one or two and give learners time to think.
1. **Trouble spots:** listen for these misunderstandings and use the suggested correction.
1. **Evidence and safety:** observe only what you are prepared to review against the agreed criteria.
1. **Review and bridge:** ask learners to complete the ungraded recall prompt before reading the key takeaways, then explain why the next module follows.

The headings are deliberately repetitive. That repetition is a navigation aid for a new teacher, not content that must be memorized.

### Words you can use when you are unsure

It is safe to say:

* “Let us check that before we treat it as a fact.”
* “I do not know the current local rule; I will verify it and return to you.”
* “That is a useful question for the Advanced list. First, let us establish the beginner idea.”
* “I may be explaining this unclearly. Let me use an example.”
* “Please explain what you think happens next.”
* “Stop there. We are going to protect the learner and the information before continuing.”

Never fill a knowledge gap with a confident guess, especially for a current law, banking rule, wallet instruction, fee, or safety decision.
