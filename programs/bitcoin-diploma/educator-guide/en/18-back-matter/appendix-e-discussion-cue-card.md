# Appendix E: discussion cue card

When discussion slows:

* Can you explain that in your own words?
* Can you give a local or invented example?
* Which term needs a shared definition?
* What evidence would support that claim?
* Is that a fact, inference, preference, or prediction?
* What is the strongest alternative explanation?
* Which trade-off is missing?
* Who controls the keys, rules, records, or access?
* What changes if the internet, service, or institution fails?
* What would make a careful person stop and verify?
* Which part is globally stable and which is local?
* What remains uncertain?

When one learner dominates:

* “Let us hear from someone who has not spoken yet.”
* “Write first; then each pair will share one point.”
* “Please summarize the previous speaker before adding your view.”

When discussion becomes personal or political:

* “Let us return to the specific claim and evidence.”
* “Use an invented scenario so no one needs to disclose private experience.”
* “Which part can we verify, and which part is a value judgment?”

When a question is too advanced:

* “That belongs on our Advanced list. Which beginner concept do we need first?”
