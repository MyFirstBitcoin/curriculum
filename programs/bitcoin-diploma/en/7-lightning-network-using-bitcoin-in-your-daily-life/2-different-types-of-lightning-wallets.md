# 7.2 Different Types of Lightning Wallets

A Lightning wallet is a bit diﬀerent from a Bitcoin wallet, though it performs the same function: receiving and sending bitcoin. The diﬀerence is that a Lightning wallet allows you to send bitcoin on the Lightning Network, which itself is a second layer on top of the Bitcoin network.

Just like with Bitcoin wallets, diﬀerent Lightning wallets have diﬀerent characteristics that need to be considered before choosing one.

#### 7.2.1 Self-Custodial vs Custodial Wallets

Lightning wallets can be broken down into very specific categories but, for the sake of simplicity, we’ll divide them into two: self-custodial and custodial.

Just like Bitcoin wallets, a self-custodial Lightning wallet is one where you control the keys to the wallet, whereas a custodial Lightning wallet is one where someone else controls the keys.

When using a custodial wallet, you can send and receive payments, but you depend on someone else's permission; you’re giving up ownership of your money in exchange for convenience.

This can be an acceptable trade-oﬀ for small amounts, although it’s recommended to use a self-custodial wallet once you have an understanding of the technology.

For what follows, we’ll be talking about self-custodial Lightning wallets only.

#### 7.2.2 Open Source vs Closed Source

Just like with the Bitcoin wallets we saw in the previous chapter, Lightning wallets can be open source or closed source. Always use open-source wallets as they are completely open for review and vetting by the community.

An open-source application also means that anyone can contribute to the improvement of the software, making it a better choice for users.
